RESEARCH LITERACY FOR PEPTIDES

Learn how to evaluate peptide claims, not just read them.

Peptide Explorer is an educational research tool designed to help beginners distinguish stronger evidence from weaker evidence, understand study limitations, and learn how scientific and laboratory documentation should be interpreted.

Open the first evidence page See how the model works
Educational research tool — not medical advice. Peptide Explorer does not recommend peptide use, dosing, purchasing, or sourcing. Its purpose is to teach users how to inspect evidence and uncertainty.
WHAT THE USER SHOULD LEARN

Three skills come first.

These are the first learning outcomes behind the architecture. Features are useful only if they make these skills easier to acquire.

01 / EVIDENCE

Distinguish strong evidence from weak evidence

Learn why a cell experiment, an animal study, a two-person pilot, and a randomized human trial do not support the same level of confidence.

02 / LIMITATIONS

Understand what a study cannot prove

Look at sample size, controls, study population, endpoints, duration, replication, bias, and other factors that constrain a conclusion.

03 / LAB DOCUMENTS

Interpret laboratory documentation

Understand what COAs, HPLC, LC-MS, sterility tests, endotoxin tests, purity values, and batch information can—and cannot—tell you.

CORE INFORMATION MODEL

Start with the claim, then walk backward to the evidence.

The site is structured so that a scientific claim is never presented as an isolated fact. A user should be able to inspect the research, see how the evidence was assessed, understand the important limitations, and open the original source.

PeptideThe research subject and identity anchor.
ClaimThe exact assertion being evaluated.
StudyThe experiment or published research.
AssessmentHow much confidence the evidence supports.
Limitations + citationWhat remains uncertain and where the user can verify it.
